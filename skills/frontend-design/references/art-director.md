# Art Director

Use the shared design and implementation workflow. Spend the additional budget on choosing a better direction and refining what users will experience.

## Explore before building

Sketch two viable directions around the highest-impact open decision in the brief. Vary what leads the experience, how information is grouped, or how users move through the task. On a narrow edit, compare two treatments within the surrounding design. Use short notes or rough sketches and implement only the selected direction unless the user requests alternatives.

Compare the directions against the audience, primary task, existing brand, and available content. Choose one and define the visual rules that will carry it through the requested scope. Check whether a generic product could use the same layout and treatment by swapping in its name. If so, look for a more specific choice grounded in the product's content, vocabulary, or imagery.

## Critique the result

Review the rendered work against the brief and the chosen direction. Look at the first impression, then complete the primary task. Check representative viewport sizes, keyboard and focus behavior, and the important states or content extremes. Inspect any custom imagery, typography, or motion where it changes the experience. For a signature interaction or substantial motion changes, use [Interaction design](interaction-design.md) to test interruption, reduced motion, and the relevant input methods. If preview tools are unavailable, apply the source-based fallback in the main skill.

Use a refinement pass to fix the issues with the most impact on comprehension, usability, or visual coherence. Recheck affected areas. Finish when the requested scope is complete and the important issues are resolved or reported, with any remaining budget spent where it changes the result.
