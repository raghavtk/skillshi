# Landing Page Design

Use this workflow when the page needs to persuade a visitor or showcase work. For general product UI, use the product's existing patterns and general frontend design guidance. If both this skill and frontend-design apply, use this skill to decide the page narrative and use frontend-design for additional visual or interaction guidance when needed. This workflow also works on its own.

## Start with the visitor and the material

Read the brief and inspect the existing page, brand, and available assets. Read relevant product and design documentation, such as PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md when present. Use adjacent pages, tokens, and shared components to recover conventions when documentation is incomplete. For a focused revision, preserve the page's identity, factual content, and working behavior within the requested scope. For a new page or a redesign, choose a direction that fits what the visitor came to see.

- **Landing page.** Identify who the page is for, what is offered, and the visitor's primary next action. Make the opening answer those questions with concrete language. Show evidence early: an actual product screen, a demonstration, a product photo, or a verified result. Order subsequent sections around what the visitor needs to understand or trust before acting.
- **Portfolio.** Identify who will review the work and what decision they need to make. Lead with selected work or a clear route into it. Make each project legible through its context, the person's role, what they made, and the result when known. Keep the path to contact or other requested next steps easy to find.

Build from supplied or verified content. Leave unsupported metrics, endorsements, clients, and project outcomes out. If draft assets are needed, use honest stand-ins and identify material the user still needs to supply.

## Choose a page direction

Let the strongest available material determine the hero and the page's rhythm. A code review tool can open with an annotated diff. A photographer can open with one image from a series and let the next section introduce the project. Give each section a distinct job and choose its composition around the content. Keep related elements close, align shared edges, and use spacing and contrast to clarify priority before adding dividers or cards.

For a new or substantially redesigned page, sketch the opening and section order before implementing. If the brief leaves the direction open and the budget allows, compare two different ways to present the same material. Pick the one that makes the visitor's job clearest and build it in the existing stack. For a small change, make the relevant decision in place. Follow the brief's visual constraints and use motion only where it helps reveal work, explain the offer, or give feedback.

## Review the page

Preview the changed surface on a narrow and wide screen when possible. Check whether the opening communicates the offer or shows the work, and whether headings, images, and actions still make sense as the page narrows. Verify relevant assets load, text stays readable, controls work with keyboard and touch where supported, and links or forms reach their intended destination. A form should show success only after a successful submission. Check reduced motion if the page uses motion.

For a critique, audit, polish pass, or substantial redesign, use [Page critique and polish](references/page-review.md) to prioritize findings and choose the smallest effective change. Fix issues in scope and recheck affected areas together. For review-only requests, report findings with evidence, visitor impact, and a proposed change. If a preview is unavailable, inspect source and available checks and report what still needs rendered verification.
