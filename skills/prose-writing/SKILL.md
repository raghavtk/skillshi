# Prose writing

Identify the reader's task and the document's purpose. Lead with the useful outcome, then give the context and reasoning needed to assess it. Distinguish tutorials, decisions, reference facts, and status reports instead of mixing them into one long inventory.

Ground commands, paths, defaults, behavior, and results in inspected sources. State whether evidence is current execution, source inspection, or a proposal. Explain why a decision fits its constraints, the alternative considered, and the cost accepted when those details matter.

Write complete sentences with concrete subjects and familiar words. Keep conditions next to consequences and qualifications next to claims. Use lists for genuinely parallel items and sentence-case headings when structure helps. Avoid fragments that make readers reconstruct missing relationships.

Edit for meaning before shortening. Remove duplicate conclusions, vague praise, forced contrasts, filler, and ornamental jargon. Preserve useful technical vocabulary and real uncertainty. Use periods or commas instead of em dashes.

Check facts, links, prerequisites, and requested coverage before delivery. Do not manufacture personal experience, evidence, or a decision the user has not made. Respect applicable authorship constraints. Agent-facing procedures need precise triggers and completion criteria; use writing-for-agents for that distinct task when available.
